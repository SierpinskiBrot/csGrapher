TPS Graph Settings
TPS Graph

The average turns per second at each point of a solve, over the CFOP solves picked by the range selector.

Each point is the number of moves in that column divided by the time all of the solves spent in it.

Each move is spread evenly over the time since the previous move, so pauses show up as a low TPS.
Width

Column width, measured in % of the solve.
Smoothing

Averages each column with its neighbours, weighted by e^(-distance/smoothing) out to 3 times the smoothing, measured in % of the solve.

0 turns smoothing off.
Steps

Sectioned: each step (cross, F2L, OLL, PLL) of every solve is stretched to that step's share of the total time of the solves, so the steps line up.

Raw: every move is placed at its time divided by the solve's time.
Last layer

OLL and PLL as separate steps, or merged into one last layer step (for ZBLL and other last layer methods).
X axis

% of the solve, or time in seconds with every solve of a file stretched to that file's mean time.
Remove worst

The % of slowest solves left out.
Compare

Acubemy exports drawn on the same graph with the same settings, using all of their solves.

Create new series
aoXaverage of X

The top and bottom 5% of solves will be removed from the average.

The number of solves that will be removed is rounded up, so you cannot have an average of 2 or less.
moXmean of XComputes the mean of the last X solves, with every solve considered.

Regression Analysis Mode
Projection

How far into the future the regression will project as a multiple of the x-axis.
Offset

How far into the past the regression will begin as a multiple of the x-axis.

This affects the calculation of the regression, and can make it a better or worse fit.

The Best offset button in a regression's settings (the > button) sets the offset that gives that regression the highest R^2.
R^2

A measure from 0 to 1 of how well the regression explains the data.

For a very bad fit, this can be negative.

This is only computed after running the regression to avoid lag.
Power Law

Fits data to y = A * N^(-B) + C by least squares, with B > 0 and C ≥ 0 so times fall toward a floor that is not negative.

For a fixed B, the best A and C can be solved exactly, so only B is searched for.

Will often overestimate the time of early solves.

Power law of practice
Log-Log

Fits data to y = A * N^B using regression. Usually a good regression

Will appear as a straight line when graphed on log-log axes.
Logarithmic

Fits data to y = A + B*log(N+1) using regression. This is often the best regression.

Will appear as a straight line when graphed with a log y-axis
